[Cambridge Game Jam](https://camgamejam.com) gives you a theme and roughly 48 hours to build a game around it. The 2026 edition ran from 27 February to 1 March, with the theme **Set in Stone**.

Friday evening has the opening ceremony and the theme reveal. You build across Saturday and most of Sunday. Judging happens on Sunday from 16:00 to 18:00, then everyone plays each other's entries.

## Games, broadly defined

The brief is any game you can dream of. Video games are the common case, but board games and ARGs have been entered and judged on equal terms. If you can explain the rules and someone can play it, it counts.

This matters for team-building, because a good jam team is rarely all programmers. Artists, sound designers and writers carry a lot of the result, and the organisers actively encourage bringing in friends from other subjects. A board game entry might involve no code at all.

## Who can enter

All current Cambridge students, from any discipline. The organisers make a point of keeping the event welcoming to people with no background in games, and first-time jammers make up a good share of every year's entries.

## Sponsors

The 2026 jam was backed by Anthropic, Jane Street, Qube RT, Sierra, Iconic Games, Optiver and Citadel.

## Advice from people who have done it

Game jams punish ambition more than any other format, because a game has to be finished to be played at all. A half-built hackathon project still demos. A half-built game is a menu screen.

- Pick something you could finish by Saturday lunchtime, then spend the rest of the time making it good. Everyone overscopes on their first jam.
- Use an engine you already know. A jam is a poor time to learn Godot from scratch, however much you have been meaning to.
- Get something playable end to end early, even if every asset is a grey box. Placeholder art you replace on Sunday beats beautiful art attached to nothing.
- Save time for the build. Exporting, uploading and writing the submission page always takes longer than the ten minutes you allocated.
- Play the other entries afterwards. It is most of the fun, and you learn more from an hour of that than from the whole of Saturday.
