[Cam Hack](https://camhack.org) is a weekend hackathon for Cambridge students. Teams get a theme on the Saturday morning and have until Sunday afternoon to build something, then everyone presents and prizes go out.

The 2025 edition ran on 1–2 November in the Intel lab, and drew over 250 people from computer science, maths, natural sciences and engineering.

## The theme

Each year has a theme, announced at the opening ceremony. In 2025 it was **Unintended Behaviour**.

That choice was deliberate. Most hackathons push teams towards a pitch: a business case, a market, a demo that could plausibly become a startup. Cam Hack pushes the other way and asks what happens when you optimise for absurdity. The results in 2025 bear that out.

**Keyboard²** won by turning a piano into a typewriter, mapping chord progressions onto English letter frequencies so that typing became a matter of playing the right chords. **Doom Scrolling** rendered the original DOOM in real time using looping TikToks. **The Mis-Interpreter** got C code executing inside Minecraft and Mindustry. **Compound Mapper** mapped molecular structures onto real roads in Albuquerque, so you could drive the shape of a caffeine molecule.

The write-ups and code from 2025 are on the [Devpost project gallery](https://cam-hack-2025.devpost.com/project-gallery).

## Who can enter

You need to be a current University of Cambridge student. Teams go up to five people, and entering on your own is allowed. Beginners are welcome and turn up every year. The theme levels the field, since nobody has a head start on building a piano typewriter.

There is no requirement to be a computer scientist. Artists, writers and people who mainly want to name things have all been useful team members.

## What you get

Food across both days, plenty of free stash, and mini-events scattered through the weekend to break up the building. The winning team in 2025 took £200 per person plus a choice of prize, with smartwatches, headphones and vouchers on the table.

## Sponsors

The 2025 hackathon was supported by Anthropic, Entrepreneur First, Jane Street, Founders at the University of Cambridge, Qube RT, Sierra and TPP UK. Several of these also sponsor the society directly, and their pages are under [Sponsors](/wiki/sponsors).

## Turning up prepared

A few things worth knowing before the morning of:

- Sort your team in advance if you can. Team formation on the day works, but it eats an hour you would rather spend building.
- Have a working development environment before you arrive. Installing a toolchain on hackathon wifi is a bad start.
- Scope down hard. The projects that finish are the ones whose creators cut two thirds of the original idea by Saturday evening.
- Sleep at some point. The 3am code is rarely the code that demos well.

Registration opens in Michaelmas and gets announced on the [mailing list](https://lists.cam.ac.uk/sympa/subscribe/soc-cucats-events) and Discord. Places have gone quickly in recent years, so sign up early.
