This section is for prospective applicants to Computer Science at Cambridge. It is written by students who went through the process recently, and it tries to answer the questions that the official pages tend to leave implicit — what the parts of the application are actually for, and what you can usefully do about each of them.

> [!WARNING]
> We are students, not admissions officers. Nothing here is official, and requirements change between cycles. Confirm every detail against the [official course page](https://www.undergraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/courses/computer-science) and the [Department of Computer Science and Technology](https://www.cst.cam.ac.uk/admissions/undergraduate) before you rely on it.

## What the application consists of

There are more moving parts than most applicants expect, and they are assessed together rather than as a checklist:

1. **Your UCAS application**, including predicted grades and your personal statement. Cambridge sits on the earlier UCAS deadline in mid-October, not the January one that most courses use — check the [current deadline on UCAS](https://www.ucas.com/) and work backwards from it.
2. **A Cambridge-specific application form**, which you complete after your UCAS application and which asks for more detail about your qualifications and module marks.
3. **The TMUA**, a mathematics admissions test you must register for separately and well in advance. See [Admissions Test](/wiki/applying-to-cambridge/admissions-test).
4. **Interviews**, usually in December, for the majority of applicants. See [Interview Preparation](/wiki/applying-to-cambridge/interview-prep).
5. **Your school reference and, where relevant, contextual data** about your school and background.

You will not normally be asked to submit written work for Computer Science.

## Entry requirements

The published minimum offer level is **A\*A\*A** at A-level, or **41–42 points with 776 at Higher Level** for the IB. Colleges commonly specify the A\* in Mathematics and/or Further Mathematics, and some set conditions beyond the minimum.

On subjects:

- **Mathematics is required**, at A-level or IB Higher Level. For the IB, Analysis and Approaches is the requested option.
- **Further Mathematics is expected to AS or A level if your school offers it.** If your school does not offer it, that is explicitly accounted for and is not held against you — but say so on your application.
- Physics and Computer Science are useful and common, but not required. Computer Science A-level in particular is neither expected nor a substitute for the mathematics.

The honest summary is that Cambridge Computer Science is a mathematically demanding course and the requirements reflect that. If you enjoy the problem-solving parts of mathematics more than the programming parts, you are the right shape for it.

## Choosing a College

You apply to a College as well as to the University. All Colleges teach the same Tripos, sit the same exams and award the same degree, so this matters much less than applicants fear — but two details are worth knowing:

- **Peterhouse and Trinity additionally require the Computer Sciences Aptitude Test (CSAT).** If you would rather sit only one admissions test, that is a real consideration.
- Some Colleges set higher or additional entry conditions than the published minimum.

Beyond that, pick on things you can actually judge: location, accommodation, size, and whether you liked the place when you visited — the [list of Colleges](https://www.undergraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/colleges) is the place to start. You can also make an open application and be allocated a College instead. Being placed in the Winter Pool, where other Colleges reconsider your application after interviews, is normal and results in a good number of offers each year.

## Rough timeline

| When                       | What happens                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Spring and summer          | Open days, subject reading, start drafting the personal statement, register for the TMUA when registration opens |
| Late September             | TMUA registration closes — this deadline is hard and easy to miss                                                |
| Mid-October                | UCAS deadline for Cambridge; complete the Cambridge application form shortly after                               |
| October                    | Sit the TMUA                                                                                                     |
| November to early December | Interview invitations sent out                                                                                   |
| Early to mid-December      | Interviews                                                                                                       |
| Late January               | Decisions released                                                                                               |

Exact dates move every year. Treat the table as shape, not schedule, and confirm each date officially.

## What actually helps

Two things, in our experience.

**Do some mathematics beyond your syllabus.** Not because it impresses anyone, but because the TMUA and the interviews both test whether you can apply what you know to unfamiliar problems, and there is no way to develop that except by trying unfamiliar problems. Past TMUA papers and STEP questions are good sources even though STEP is not required.

**Build or read something you find genuinely interesting, then think about it properly.** One project or one book you can discuss with real understanding is worth far more than a list of ten you skimmed. This pays off in both the personal statement and the interview.

What helps less than people think: collecting programming languages, paid interview coaching (the University explicitly does not endorse it), and trying to guess what admissions tutors want to hear.
