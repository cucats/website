## Course material

The Department publishes almost everything openly, which is unusual and worth exploiting.

- [Part IA course pages](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/current/part1a.html) carry lecture notes, slides and supervision exercises for every first-year course, publicly. Equivalent pages exist for Part IB and Part II.
- [Past exam papers](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/exams/pastpapers/) go back decades. This is the most useful revision resource there is, and it repays looking at early in a course over saving it for Easter term.
- [Department teaching pages](https://www.cst.cam.ac.uk/teaching) give the official overview of the Tripos and its structure.
- Moodle is where course announcements, ticks and submissions live once you have arrived.

For reference, the Part IA courses in 2025–26:

| Term       | Courses                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Michaelmas | Foundations of Computer Science, Object-Oriented Programming, Discrete Mathematics, Digital Electronics, Databases, Introduction to Graphics, plus OCaml and hardware practical classes |
| Lent       | Algorithms 1 and 2, Operating Systems, Machine Learning and Real-world Data, Discrete Mathematics continued                                                                             |
| Easter     | Introduction to Probability, Software and Security Engineering, Interaction Design                                                                                                      |

Two languages carry the first year: OCaml for Foundations of Computer Science and Java for Object-Oriented Programming. Neither is assumed knowledge on arrival, and we have pages on [setting up OCaml](/wiki/tutorials/ocaml-setup) and [getting started with Java](/wiki/tutorials/java-basics).

> [!TIP]
> Course lists change between years. Check the current course pages before trusting that table.

## Computing facilities

- [The SRCF](https://www.srcf.net/), the Student-Run Computing Facility, is a volunteer-run society providing free web hosting, shell accounts, mailing lists, email and hosted chat to Cambridge students and staff. Membership costs nothing, and [Hosting on the SRCF](/wiki/tutorials/hosting-on-the-srcf) walks through using it.
- [The MCS](https://help.uis.cam.ac.uk/service/desktop-services/mcs), the Managed Cluster Service, covers the shared desktop machines across the University and in many Colleges, which are useful when you want a machine with the course software already on it.
- University Information Services is the source of truth for university accounts, VPN access and software licensing.

## Free tools for students

- [GitHub Student Developer Pack](https://education.github.com/pack) bundles a large set of developer tools free while you are a student, including GitHub Pro. Verify with your university email.
- [JetBrains student licences](https://www.jetbrains.com/community/education/#students) cover the full suite with academic verification.

Both take a few minutes and are worth doing in your first week.

## Competitive programming and puzzles

- [UCCPS](https://uccps.soc.srcf.net) is the Cambridge competitive programming society, with regular practice sessions and contests.
- [Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/) is the main international contest platform, with a large archive sorted by difficulty.
- [AtCoder](https://atcoder.jp/) runs beginner-friendly contests with well-written problems.
- [Advent of Code](https://adventofcode.com/) is a daily December puzzle, approachable in any language.
- [CUCaTS Codevent](/wiki/events/codevent) is our own December competition.

[Getting Started with Competitive Programming](/wiki/tutorials/competitive-programming) covers how to approach all of these.

## Other societies

- [CyberSoc](https://cybersoc.soc.srcf.net) covers security and cryptography, including CTF competitions.
- [UCCPS](https://uccps.soc.srcf.net) covers competitive programming, as above.

We co-run events with both, so their announcement channels are worth joining alongside ours.

## Careers

- [Our sponsors](/wiki/sponsors) fund the society, and most of them recruit Cambridge students directly. Talks, workshops and dinners get announced on our mailing list.
- [University Careers Service](https://www.careers.cam.ac.uk/) does free CV reviews, application advice and internship listings. Computer scientists who assume they do not need it are the people who most often do.

Internship applications open earlier than most people expect, often in the September or October beforehand, with popular schemes closing well before Christmas. Hoping for a first-year internship means looking in Michaelmas.
