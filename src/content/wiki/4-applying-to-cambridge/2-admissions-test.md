Every Cambridge Computer Science applicant sits the **Test of Mathematics for University Admission**, the TMUA. Mathematics and Economics applicants sit it too, so there is plenty of material about it around.

Before anything else on this page:

> [!WARNING]
> You must register in advance and sit the test at an authorised centre. Registration usually closes at the end of September, with the test in October. Your school may not be a centre, and may not register you automatically. Check yourself. Missing registration stops your application, and there is no way around it.

Official information lives at [esat-tmua.ac.uk](https://esat-tmua.ac.uk/about-the-tests/tmua-test/) and on the [Cambridge admissions tests page](https://www.undergraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/applying/admissions-tests). Confirm this cycle's dates there.

## Format

|             | TMUA                                                    |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Papers      | 2                                                       |
| Questions   | 20 multiple-choice per paper                            |
| Time        | 75 minutes per paper, 2 hours 30 minutes total          |
| Calculators | Not permitted                                           |
| Delivery    | Computer-based, at an authorised test centre            |
| Score       | One overall score from 1.0 to 9.0, to one decimal place |

Paper 1, Mathematical Thinking, tests applying mathematical knowledge in unfamiliar situations. Paper 2, Mathematical Reasoning, tests constructing and analysing mathematical arguments: proof, logic, necessary and sufficient conditions, and spotting where an argument goes wrong.

That works out to roughly 3 minutes 45 seconds a question, which shapes how you should prepare more than anything else about the test.

## How the score gets used

Colleges use TMUA results to help decide who to interview. There is no pass mark, though a strong score is normally needed to reach interview, and the score gets weighed again alongside everything else when offers are decided.

It is one input among several. A very strong score will not carry a weak application, and one disappointing paper will not automatically end a strong one.

## What it covers

The syllabus is deliberately narrow: roughly AS-level pure mathematics, plus a strand on logic, proof and reasoning that goes past most A-level courses. No mechanics, no statistics beyond elementary probability, and nothing from Further Maths that would disadvantage applicants whose schools do not offer it.

Read the official content specification linked from the [TMUA site](https://esat-tmua.ac.uk/about-the-tests/tmua-test/) and treat it as exhaustive, because it is. Knowing what is absent saves you time.

Most applicants have never been taught the reasoning strand explicitly, so it repays deliberate study. Make sure you are fluent with necessary and sufficient conditions, converse and contrapositive and which of them are equivalent, proof by contradiction, proof by induction, disproof by counterexample, and finding the invalid step in a plausible-looking argument.

## Two habits that gain marks

Exploit the multiple choice. Nobody marks your working, so a full solution is often wasted effort. Substituting a convenient value, checking a sign or testing a limiting case will frequently kill four of five options in seconds.

If a question asks how many real solutions a quadratic has, you do not need to solve it. For $x^2 + 4x + 5 = 0$ the discriminant is $16 - 20 = -4$, so there are none, and that is one line.

Then watch the domain. TMUA distractors get built from the errors candidates actually make, and forgetting to check a solution is valid is the most common. Take:

$$
\log_2 x + \log_2 (x - 2) = 3
$$

Combining gives $x(x-2) = 8$, so $x^2 - 2x - 8 = 0$ and $(x-4)(x+2) = 0$, giving $x = 4$ or $x = -2$. But $x = -2$ makes both logarithms undefined, so only $x = 4$ survives. Both values will be on the option list, and so will "two solutions".

The general lesson: whatever falls out of the algebra is not always the answer to the question asked.

## Preparing

Work through the past papers, which are free on the official site and are the best resource by a distance. Do them under real timing, because pace is half of what is being tested.

Practise without a calculator now. Mental arithmetic, surds, exponent manipulation and cancelling before multiplying all need to be automatic, and this is what candidates most often leave too late.

Review your wrong answers properly. For each one, work out whether you misread the question, slipped in arithmetic, or had a genuine gap. Those three need completely different fixes and lumping them together wastes the review.

STEP and Maths Olympiad questions make good supplementary practice for unfamiliar problems, though neither is required here.

## The CSAT

Apply to **Peterhouse or Trinity** and you will also sit the Computer Sciences Aptitude Test. Those Colleges set the arrangements, so check with them directly, and factor it into your College choice.

## On the day

Both papers happen on the same day. Arrive early, take permitted ID, and expect the on-screen interface to feel slightly unfamiliar. The official practice materials use the same platform, so use them at least once so the format is not a surprise.

There is no negative marking, so never leave a question blank.
