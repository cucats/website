Computer Science applicants sit the TMUA, as do applicants in Mathematics and Economics.

> [!WARNING]
> Registration is separate from your application, closes at the end of September, and happens at an authorised centre. Your school may not be one and may not register you. Missing it stops the application with no recourse.

Current dates and the content specification are at [esat-tmua.ac.uk](https://esat-tmua.ac.uk/about-the-tests/tmua-test/) and on the [Cambridge admissions tests page](https://www.undergraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/applying/admissions-tests).

## Structure

|             | TMUA                          |
| ----------- | ----------------------------- |
| Papers      | 2                             |
| Questions   | 20 multiple-choice per paper  |
| Time        | 75 minutes per paper          |
| Calculators | Not permitted                 |
| Score       | 1.0 to 9.0, one decimal place |

Paper 1 is Mathematical Thinking, applying known technique to unfamiliar configurations. Paper 2 is Mathematical Reasoning, which is where the test differs from anything on an A-level syllabus: proof structure, the logic of conditionals, and locating the invalid step in an argument that reaches a true conclusion.

Colleges use the score to decide interview invitations, and it is weighed again against everything else at the offer stage. There is no threshold published because there is not one.

## The reasoning strand

Necessary and sufficient conditions, the equivalence of a conditional with its contrapositive and the non-equivalence with its converse, proof by contradiction and by induction, and disproof by counterexample. Questions in this strand frequently present a proof and ask which step fails, where the conclusion is true and the argument for it is not.

The distinction that carries the most marks is between a statement, its converse and its contrapositive, applied under quantifiers. Negating a statement with alternating quantifiers is worth being fluent at, since care alone is slow under timing.

## Where the marks go

Working is not marked, so a full solution is frequently wasted effort. Substituting a convenient value, testing a limiting case, or checking a sign eliminates most of an option list in seconds.

The number of real roots of $x^2 + 4x + 5$ follows from the discriminant being $-4$, and nothing further is required.

Domain conditions are where the distractors live. In

$$
\log_2 x + \log_2 (x - 2) = 3
$$

combining gives $x^2 - 2x - 8 = 0$ and roots $4$ and $-2$, and only $4$ lies in the domain. The option list will contain $-2$, and it will contain "two solutions".

The general pattern: the distractors are generated from the specific errors candidates make, so an answer that appears without effort is worth one look at what you might have skipped.

## Preparation

Past papers under timing, which is roughly 3 minutes 45 seconds per question and is the binding constraint for most people.

Calculator-free fluency in surds, indices, logarithms and factorisation, since the arithmetic is designed to be tractable by hand and is only tractable if that fluency is automatic.

Wrong answers separate into misreads, arithmetic slips and genuine gaps, and the three take different remedies. Aggregating them into one number tells you nothing.

STEP and olympiad material transfers well for the unfamiliar-configuration half, and neither is required for this course.

## The CSAT

Peterhouse and Trinity additionally require the Computer Sciences Aptitude Test. Those Colleges set the arrangements, so ask them, and factor it into where you apply.

## On the day

Both papers sit on the same day. There is no negative marking, so nothing is left blank.
