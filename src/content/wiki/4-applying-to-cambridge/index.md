This section is for people applying to Computer Science at Cambridge. Students who went through it recently wrote it, and the aim is to explain what each part of the application is for, which the official pages tend to leave implicit.

> [!WARNING]
> We are students, so none of this is official, and requirements move between cycles. Check every detail against the [course page](https://www.undergraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/courses/computer-science) and the [Department of Computer Science and Technology](https://www.cst.cam.ac.uk/admissions/undergraduate) before relying on it.

## What you actually submit

There are more moving parts than most applicants expect, and they get assessed together.

1. Your UCAS application, with predicted grades and your personal statement. Cambridge sits on the earlier mid-October UCAS deadline, so work backwards from the [date UCAS publishes](https://www.ucas.com/).
2. A Cambridge-specific application form, completed after UCAS, asking for more detail on your qualifications and module marks.
3. The TMUA, which needs separate registration well in advance. See [Admissions Test](/wiki/applying-to-cambridge/admissions-test).
4. Interviews in December for most applicants. See [Interview Preparation](/wiki/applying-to-cambridge/interview-prep).
5. Your school reference, along with contextual data about your school and background where it applies.

Computer Science does not normally ask for written work.

## Entry requirements

The published minimum offer is **A\*A\*A** at A-level, or **41–42 points with 776 at Higher Level** for the IB. Colleges commonly attach the A\* to Mathematics or Further Mathematics, and some set conditions above the minimum.

On subjects:

- Mathematics is required, at A-level or IB Higher Level. IB applicants are asked for Analysis and Approaches.
- Further Mathematics is expected to AS or A level where your school offers it. Where your school does not, that gets taken into account, though you should say so on your application.
- Physics and Computer Science are common and useful without being required. Computer Science A-level in particular does no work as a substitute for the mathematics.

The requirements say something real about the course. Cambridge Computer Science is mathematically demanding from the first term, and people who enjoy the problem-solving side of maths more than the programming side tend to do well on it.

## Choosing a College

You apply to a College as well as to the University. Every College teaches the same Tripos, sits the same exams and awards the same degree, so this decision carries less weight than applicants fear. Two details do matter:

Peterhouse and Trinity also require the Computer Sciences Aptitude Test (CSAT). If sitting one admissions test appeals more than sitting two, that is a real consideration.

Some Colleges attach higher or additional conditions to their offers than the published minimum.

Otherwise, choose on things you can judge: location, accommodation, size, and whether you liked the place when you visited. The [list of Colleges](https://www.undergraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/colleges) is the place to start. Making an open application and being allocated a College is also fine.

Ending up in the Winter Pool, where other Colleges reconsider your application after interviews, is normal and produces a good number of offers each year.

## Rough timeline

| When                       | What happens                                                                                        |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Spring and summer          | Open days, subject reading, first drafts of the personal statement, TMUA registration once it opens |
| Late September             | TMUA registration closes. This deadline is hard and easy to miss                                    |
| Mid-October                | UCAS deadline, then the Cambridge form shortly after                                                |
| October                    | Sit the TMUA                                                                                        |
| November to early December | Interview invitations                                                                               |
| Early to mid-December      | Interviews                                                                                          |
| Late January               | Decisions                                                                                           |

Every one of these dates moves between years, so treat the table as shape and confirm the specifics officially.

## What helps

Two things, from our experience.

Do some mathematics past your syllabus. The reason is practical: both the TMUA and the interviews test whether you can apply what you know to problems you have not seen, and the only way to get better at that is to attempt problems you have not seen. Past TMUA papers and STEP questions both work, and STEP is not required for this course.

Then build or read something you find genuinely interesting and think about it properly. One project or one book you understand well is worth more than ten you skimmed, and it pays off twice, in the personal statement and again at interview.

Things that help less than people expect: collecting programming languages, paid interview coaching, and trying to guess what admissions tutors want to hear.
