Practical guides written by CUCaTS members. The section is deliberately open-ended: if someone had to work it out, it is worth writing down.

Having explained something to a friend more than twice is a good sign it belongs here. [Contributing](/wiki#writing-a-page) covers how to add a page.

## What makes a good one

The guides here that people actually use share a few properties.

They solve one problem. "The OCaml toolchain" is a tutorial; "programming languages" is a textbook.

They are runnable, so every command works if you paste it in order. They say what the result should look like, so a reader can tell whether they are on track.

They cover the failure modes. The paragraph explaining the error message you lost an hour to is usually the most valuable thing on the page.

And they date themselves where it matters, because tooling rots. If a guide depends on a particular version, say so.

Length is no virtue. A page that saves someone twenty minutes has earned its place.
