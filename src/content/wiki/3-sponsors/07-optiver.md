Optiver is a global market maker. It quotes buy and sell prices continuously across financial markets, using its own capital to provide liquidity. Founded in Amsterdam in 1986, it now runs from more than a dozen financial centres including London, Chicago, New York, Hong Kong, Singapore and Sydney.

The scale is worth stating because it explains the engineering. Optiver executes over 10 million trades a day, connects to more than 100 exchanges, prices over a million instruments, and processes petabytes of market data daily. That workload runs on low-latency C++ and FPGA hardware, alongside large-scale data platforms and a growing amount of machine learning.

## What market making actually is

The term does a poor job of explaining itself. A market maker offers to both buy and sell an instrument at all times, quoting a price on each side. It earns the spread between the two, and takes on the risk of holding whatever it has just bought. Doing that profitably across a million instruments is a problem in pricing, risk and latency all at once, which is why these firms hire so many engineers.

## The Cambridge Trading Academy

Optiver runs the Optiver & Cambridge Trading Academy, an eight-session course led by people from the firm. It goes from options theory through to designing trading algorithms in Python, and finishes with pairs competing on Optibook, Optiver's simulated market that runs around the clock. There is a prize for the winners.

You can [register interest](https://optiver.com/recruitment-events/save-the-date-the-optiver-cambridge-trading-academy/) to hear when applications open. It is one of the better things on offer through our sponsors, because you come out of it having actually written a trading algorithm.
