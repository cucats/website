A signposting page. Everything here is either free or free to Cambridge students, and everything has been checked to still exist — but the internet rots, so if you find a dead link, please fix it.

## Course material

The Department publishes almost everything openly, which is genuinely unusual and worth exploiting.

- **[Part IA course pages](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/current/part1a.html)** — lecture notes, slides and supervision exercises for every first-year course, publicly available. The equivalent pages exist for Part IB and Part II.
- **[Past exam papers](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/exams/pastpapers/)** — the whole archive, going back decades. The single most useful revision resource there is, and worth looking at early in a course rather than only in Easter term.
- **[Department teaching pages](https://www.cst.cam.ac.uk/teaching)** — the official overview of the Tripos and its structure.
- **Moodle** — where course-specific announcements, ticks and submissions actually live once you have arrived.

For reference, the Part IA courses in 2025–26 are:

| Term       | Courses                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Michaelmas | Foundations of Computer Science, Object-Oriented Programming, Discrete Mathematics, Digital Electronics, Databases, Introduction to Graphics, plus OCaml and hardware practical classes |
| Lent       | Algorithms 1 and 2, Operating Systems, Machine Learning and Real-world Data, Discrete Mathematics (continued)                                                                           |
| Easter     | Introduction to Probability, Software and Security Engineering, Interaction Design                                                                                                      |

Two languages carry the first year: **OCaml** for Foundations of Computer Science and **Java** for Object-Oriented Programming. Neither is assumed knowledge on arrival.

> [!TIP]
> Course lists and content change between years. Check the current course pages rather than trusting this table.

## Computing facilities

- **[The SRCF](https://www.srcf.net/)** — the Student-Run Computing Facility, a volunteer-run society providing free web hosting, shell accounts, mailing lists, email and hosted chat to Cambridge students and staff. Membership costs nothing and it is the natural place to host a project or a society website. Several Cambridge societies run on it, including some linked from this wiki.
- **[The MCS](https://help.uis.cam.ac.uk/service/desktop-services/mcs)** — the University's Managed Cluster Service, the shared desktop machines available across the University and in many Colleges. Useful when you need a machine with the course software already installed.
- **University Information Services** — the source of truth for university accounts, VPN access, and software licensing available to students.

## Free tools for students

- **[GitHub Student Developer Pack](https://education.github.com/pack)** — a large bundle of developer tools free while you are a student, including GitHub Pro and a long list of third-party services. Verify with your university email.
- **[JetBrains student licences](https://www.jetbrains.com/community/education/#students)** — the full JetBrains suite, free with academic verification.

Both take a few minutes to set up and are worth doing in your first week.

## Competitive programming and puzzles

- **[UCCPS](https://uccps.soc.srcf.net)** — the Cambridge competitive programming society. Regular practice sessions and contests, and the right place to start if algorithmic problem-solving appeals.
- **[Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/)** — the main international contest platform, with a large archive of problems by difficulty.
- **[AtCoder](https://atcoder.jp/)** — regular beginner-friendly contests with well-written problems.
- **[Advent of Code](https://adventofcode.com/)** — a daily puzzle through December, approachable in any language, and a genuinely enjoyable way to learn a new one.
- **[CUCaTS Codevent](https://cc.cucats.org/about)** — our own December puzzle competition, with prizes.

## Other societies

- **[CyberSoc](https://cybersoc.soc.srcf.net)** — security and cryptography, including CTF competitions.
- **[UCCPS](https://uccps.soc.srcf.net)** — competitive programming, as above.

We co-run events with both, so joining their announcement channels is worthwhile alongside ours.

## Careers

- **[Our sponsors](/sponsors)** — the companies that fund the society, most of which recruit Cambridge students directly. Sponsor talks, workshops and dinners are announced on our mailing list.
- **[University Careers Service](https://www.careers.cam.ac.uk/)** — free CV reviews, application advice and an internship listings service. Underused by computer scientists who assume they do not need it.

Internship applications for the following summer open earlier than most people expect — often in the September or October before, with popular schemes closing well before Christmas. If you are hoping for a first-year internship, start looking in Michaelmas rather than after exams.
